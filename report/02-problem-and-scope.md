# 2. Scenario interpretation

## 2.1 Organisation
Atlantic Renewable Care is a small maintenance provider for solar photovoltaic,small 
wind and battery installations across domestic agricultural and commercial sites (Facts:1 and 2).Its work covers planned 
inspections fault response and approved repairs.
## 2.2 Current process
Faults are reported by telephone, email or an online enquiry form.Office staff make an initial record
and may ask for readings or photographs (Facts: 13-15). A technical lead decides whether the issue needs remote advice,
a routine visit or urgent attendance. (Facts: 16)

Technicians have different skills and experience, some visits require multiple 
skills, two technicians or specialist access equipment. (Facts: 20-21) Travel time and weather conditions affect scheduling. (Fact: 22)

Customers may need to approve a quotation,approval sometimes obtained by telephone, and staff record it.(Facts: 26-27)

Technicians record observations, readings, completed work, parts used and photographs, some readings are entered later from paper notes.(Fact: 28-30)
Everything about visit are stored in separate spreadsheets, email folders and technicians’ notebooks.(Fact: 5)

## 2.3 Problems and consequences
1. Visits are booked without confirming that the technician holds the required authorisation and that the correct parts are available ->
The technician arrives at site but cannot carry out the work, so the visit is wasted.
2. Customers report symptoms rather than the actual fault, so the required skills and parts may not be known when the visit is planned ->
The wrong technician or parts may be sent, resulting in an unproductive visit.
3. Information about completed work is passed from technicians to the office with a delay, as some records are still on paper ->
   The office works with out-of-date visit status information.
4. Customers do not receive clear and timely information about the safety of their system and outstanding work ->
   Customers may continue to use potentially unsafe equipment.
5. There is no clearly defined process for confirming that equipment is safe to return to service after a repair ->
   Equipment may be returned to service without all required checks being completed.
6. Parts records are not updated when parts are reallocated between jobs ->
   Recorded stock levels become inaccurate, which can delay repairs.
## 2.4 Desired outcomes
1. Visits are booked only when a suitably authorised technician and the required parts are confirmed **Problem 1**
2. The actual fault is confirmed and recorded after inspection and linked to the symptoms originally reported. **Problem 2**
3. The office receives timely, up-to-date information when visits are completed. **Problem 3**
4. Customers receive timely, verified information about the safety of their system and any outstanding work. **Problem 4**
5. Equipment returns to service only after the required checks are completed and an authorised person approves the result. **Problem 5**
6. All use and reallocation of parts is recorded promptly, so stock records remain accurate. **Problem 6**

## 2.5 Evidence and uncertainty
This analysis is based on CA1 Scenario 08: Renewable Energy Maintenance Service and identifies
41 facts (F-01–F-41).
The evidence also includes statements from six stakeholders, 
which highlight practical problems with technician authorisation,
fault identification, visit records, safety decisions and spare parts
management. The main uncertainties concern fault prioritisation and service 
boundaries (Q-01), acceptable evidence of quotation approval (Q-05),
authorisation for return to service (Q-12), and access to site information 
and customer-facing technical details (Q-16, Q-17).
To allow the analysis to proceed, temporary assumptions (A-01) have been proposed,
for example on how telephone approval is recorded, who authorises
return to service and how technicians work without a stable connection.
These assumptions are not confirmed company policies and must be validated
with the relevant stakeholders. The complete facts, assumptions and questions
tables are provided in Appendix A.

# 3. Purpose, scope and boundary

## 3.1 Purpose
The purpose of the system is to help ARC coordinate maintenance of 
renewable energy installations by managing fault reports, technician scheduling,
spare parts, service visits and return-to-service approvals, so that work is completed efficiently,
equipment returns to service only after the required checks, records remain accurate,
and customers receive reliable updates on the status and safety of their installations

## 3.2 In scope
1. Recording and managing customers, sites, installations and components, including site access information with restricted visibility (P-07)
2. Recording fault reports, assessing faults and tracking their status (P-02, P-04)
3. Supporting fault prioritisation and linking related reports, findings and work (P-02)
4. Coordinating technician scheduling based on skills, authorisations, availability and site conditions (P-01)
5. Managing spare parts availability, allocation and usage records (P-01, P-06)
6. Recording service agreement coverage, quotations and customer approvals (P-07)
7. Recording maintenance visits, inspection findings, work completed and visit outcomes (P-03)
8. Managing required safety checks and return-to-service approval (P-04, P-05)
9. Providing a management overview of open faults, overdue inspections, technician workload, parts delays and pending approvals (O-07, F-36)
10. Providing controlled progress updates and reports to customers (P-04)


## 3.3 Out of scope
**Live energy monitoring** – Explicitly excluded in the scenario (F-41).\
**Equipment control** – Explicitly excluded in the scenario (F-41).\
**Customer billing systems** – Explicitly excluded in the scenario (F-41).\
**Emergency electrical response** – ARC does not provide this service; safety concerns are escalated instead (F-17).\
**Installation and commissioning of new systems** – The scenario describes maintenance only; it is not stated whether ARC still installs systems. Treated as out of scope until confirmed.

## 3.4 Dependencies, assumptions and constraints
**Dependencies**

1. External suppliers and manufacturers: Availability and delivery of spare parts, including parts supplied under warranty, depend on external organisations (F-23).
2. Technicians' mobile devices: Technicians use phones or tablets to access and record maintenance information (F-38).
3. Customer cooperation: Scheduling and non-covered maintenance work depend on site access arrangements and customer approval of quotations (F-12, F-26).
4. Stakeholder decisions: The system depends on the service manager and technical lead confirming business rules for fault priorities, technician authorisations and return-to-service approval (Q-01, Q-07, Q-12).

**Assumptions**

The analysis relies on temporary assumptions A-01–A-14, listed in Appendix A, which must be confirmed with stakeholders.

**Constraints**

1. Unreliable connectivity: The system must allow for poor internet connectivity at rural and remote sites (F-38).
2. Defined project boundaries: Live energy monitoring, equipment control and customer billing are excluded from the project (F-41; see 3.3).
3. Small company resources: ARC is a small company, so the system should remain practical to implement, operate and maintain (F-01).
4. Existing incomplete records: Some installations lack model, serial number, warranty or service history information (F-07, F-08), which limits the completeness of historical data.
5. Different access requirements: Office staff, technicians, managers and customers require different levels of access to operational, technical and site-access information (F-12, F-35, F-39).
