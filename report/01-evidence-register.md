## 1. Facts
   Organisation and current situation
1.	Atlantic Renewable Care is a small company that maintains solar photovoltaic systems, small wind turbines and battery installations.
2.	The company serves residential properties, farms and business premises.
3.	The company performs planned inspections, responds to reported faults and carries out approved maintenance work.
4.	The company does not install every system that it currently maintains.
5.	Customer details, installation notes, visits and quotations are stored in separate spreadsheets, email folders and technicians’ notebooks.
      Installations and service agreements
6.	A customer site may contain several installations, with major components supplied by different manufacturers.
7.	Model and serial number information is not always available.
8.	Warranty information and previous service history may be incomplete.
9.	Some customers have annual service agreements, while others request work only when a fault occurs.
10.	Agreements differ in the number of planned visits, included labour and expected response times.
11.	Staff sometimes rely on email correspondence to determine whether specific work is covered by an agreement.
12.	Site access requirements vary and may include a site contact, gate code, escort, roof-access equipment or confirmation that machinery has been isolated.
       Fault reports and assessment
13.	Faults are reported by telephone, email or an online enquiry form.
14.	Customers report symptoms such as reduced output, warning indicators, unusual noise, visible damage or complete loss of service.
15.	Office staff create an initial record and may request readings or photographs.
16.	A technical lead decides whether an issue requires remote advice, a routine visit or urgent attendance.
17.	The company does not provide emergency electrical response, but safety-related concerns should be escalated.
18.	Multiple reports may relate to the same underlying fault, while one inspection may identify several faults.
19.	Staff currently struggle to connect related reports, findings and work decisions.
       Technician scheduling and spare parts
20.	Technicians have different skills, experience and authorisations across solar, wind, battery and electrical work.
21.	Some visits require multiple skills, two technicians or specialist access equipment.
22.	Travel time and weather conditions affect scheduling.
23.	Spare parts may be held in stock, ordered for a specific job or supplied by a manufacturer under warranty.
24.	Staff sometimes schedule repairs before confirming that all required parts have arrived.
25.	During a visit, a technician may complete a temporary repair, identify additional work or be unable to proceed because access or site conditions are unsuitable.
26.	Customers may need to approve a quotation before work not covered by their agreement begins.
27.	Approval is sometimes obtained by telephone, and staff record it inconsistently.
       Inspection, repair and return to service
28.	Technicians record observations, readings, completed work, parts used and photographs.
29.	Reporting forms differ depending on the type of technology.
30.	Some readings are entered later from paper notes, making it difficult for office staff to determine whether a visit is complete.
31.	A visit does not necessarily resolve the fault.
32.	Possible outcomes include no fault found, further diagnosis required, awaiting parts, temporary operation, repair completed or equipment taken out of service.
33.	Before an installation returns to normal service, required checks should be completed and the result accepted by an authorised person.
34.	It has not been decided whether the technician who completed the repair may always authorise the return to service.
35.	Customers need clear reports, but commercial notes and internal safety discussions should not automatically be included in customer reports.
       System requirements and organisational context
36.	The service manager needs information about open faults, overdue inspections, technician workload, parts delays, repeat visits, unresolved safety concerns and installations awaiting return-to-service approval.
37.	Customers want progress updates without receiving unverified technical conclusions.
38.	Technicians use phones or tablets, but connectivity at rural and remote sites can be poor.
39.	Office staff need access to schedules and customer communications, while technicians need only the site and technical information relevant to their assigned work.
40.	The project scope includes maintenance coordination and record-keeping.
41.	Live energy monitoring, equipment control and customer billing systems are outside the project scope.
## 2. Assumptions
1. Centralised information system. It is assumed that the new system will provide a central location for records currently stored in spreadsheets, emails and notebooks.
2. Fault statuses. Each fault will have a status to track its progress from initial reporting to resolution or another agreed outcome.
3. Visit statuses. Each visit will have a status distinguishing scheduled work, work in progress and visits for which records have not yet been fully submitted.
4. Skills and authorisation checks. The system will check whether a technician’s skills and authorisations match the requirements of a job before assigning the visit.
5. Parts availability checks. Before confirming a repair, the system will show whether the required parts are available or whether delivery is still pending.
6. Spare parts tracking. Every use, movement or reallocation of parts will be recorded to keep stock quantities and locations accurate.
7. Recording customer approval. Approval of quotations obtained by telephone or other methods will be recorded, including the approving person and the time of approval.
8. Separation of repair completion and return to service. Completing a repair will not automatically mean that the installation is authorised to return to normal service.
9. Role-based access control. The system will provide different access permissions for office staff, technicians, managers and customers.
10. Restricted customer reports. Customers will receive only verified information intended for external communication.
11. Offline functionality. It is assumed that technicians will be able to save relevant records without a stable internet connection and synchronise them later. The exact mechanism has not been defined.
12. Audit trail. Changes to important records, such as work statuses, stock movements and approvals, will be recorded for future review.
13. Structured service agreements. Agreement terms will be stored in a structured format so that staff can identify covered work and work requiring separate approval.
14. Fault priorities. The system will support fault priority categories, although the criteria for assigning these priorities have not yet been established.
    These are proposed working assumptions for the information system, not confirmed statements about the company’s existing policies.
